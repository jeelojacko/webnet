## 1. Land Titles Act 18
Approved group: Instrument record, examination, acceptance, and rejection
Focus: [{"sourceKey":"section:18","childLabels":["18(1)","18(2)","18(3)","18(4)"],"evidenceText":["assign a registration number, date and time","record of the acceptance of the instrument","accepted for registration","may refuse to file or register and may reject an instrument"]}]

### Official Focused Source
Registration of instrument

18(1)The registrar shall assign a registration number, date and time to every instrument received at the land titles office for filing or registration and shall enter a record of the instrument, date, time and number in the instrument record.

18(2)The instrument shall then be examined and, subject to subsection (4), a record of the acceptance of the instrument for filing or registration shall be entered in the instrument record.

18(3)When an instrument is accepted for registration pursuant to subsection (2), it shall be registered.

18(4)Notwithstanding anything in this or any other enactment, the registrar may refuse to file or register and may reject an instrument where the appropriate fee has not been paid therefor or where the instrument

(a)
does not relate to land situated in his district;

(b)
does not describe the land to which it relates by its approved parcel identifier, or is otherwise incomplete;

(b.1)
describes the land

(i)
by reference to a parcel identifier that has been marked as retired by the registrar, or

(ii)
by a description of the land in addition to a reference to its approved parcel identifier;

(c)
is not in proper form or fit for filing or registration; or

(d)
does not comply with the Community Planning Act, if applicable.

18(5)Where the registrar is required pursuant to the provisions of this Act to register the title to land or to register land or to register a person as the owner of land or to register an instrument severing or consolidating land or a transfer, the registrar shall

(a)
cancel the certificate of registered ownership, if any, as to that land; and

(b)
issue to the owner, surviving owner, personal representative, agent or trustee, as the case may be, a certificate of registered ownership for that land and, in the case of a subdivision, a certificate of registered ownership for each lot, block or parcel in the subdivision plan.

18(6)Where the registrar registers a title to land pursuant to an order under section 12, 13 or 14 or a title to previously unregistered land pursuant to section 23, the registrar shall issue and record under the Registry Act a notice in prescribed form and thereafter the title to the land shall be dealt with under this Act, and this Act applies to every instrument or document that relates to an interest in that land.

18(7)Every parcel of land in respect of which a title is registered under this Act shall be identified by its approved parcel identifier in the title register.

18(8)Repealed: 1998, c.38, s.5

18(9)Upon the rejection of an instrument, the registrar shall enter in the instrument record a record of the rejection and a memorandum of the reasons for such rejection, and shall notify the person who presented the instrument for registration.

18(10)Notwithstanding subsection (4), the registrar shall not reject an instrument referred to in subsection 12(10).

18(11)Any instrument that is filed or registered under this Act may, subject to such terms and conditions as the registrar may impose, be amended by the filing or registration of an amending instrument in prescribed form.

18(12)Notwithstanding anything in this Act, where a document sufficient to pass an estate or interest in land under the Registry Act deals with land registered under this Act, the Registrar General may, in his absolute discretion, order the proper registrar to register it under this Act, and when so registered it shall have the same effect as if registered as an instrument.

1985, c.4, s.37; 1998, c.38, s.5

Title: Instrument intake, examination, acceptance, and rejection
Main question: How does the registrar process an instrument from receipt through acceptance or rejection under section 18?
Study summary: The registrar first gives every received instrument a registration number, date, and time and records those details. The instrument is then examined; if accepted, the acceptance is recorded and the accepted instrument is registered. The registrar may refuse to file or register and may reject an instrument for the listed fee, land-location, parcel-identifier, form, completeness, or Community Planning Act compliance problems.

1. Recall the record made when an instrument is received.
Question: What must the registrar assign and record when an instrument is received?
Answer: The registrar shall assign a registration number, date, and time to every instrument received at the land titles office for filing or registration, and shall enter the instrument, date, time, and number in the instrument record.
2. Recall the examination and acceptance path.
Question: What happens after the instrument is recorded for examination?
Answer: The instrument is examined and, subject to subsection 18(4), a record of its acceptance for filing or registration is entered in the instrument record. When accepted for registration under subsection 18(2), it shall be registered.
3. Recall when the registrar may reject an instrument.
Question: On what grounds may the registrar refuse to file or register and reject an instrument?
Answer: The registrar may reject an instrument if the appropriate fee has not been paid, if it does not relate to land in the district, lacks the approved parcel identifier or is otherwise incomplete, uses a retired parcel identifier or an added description, is not in proper form or fit for filing or registration, or does not comply with the Community Planning Act if applicable.

### AI Content
Title: Subdivision public-purpose land and regulation procedure
Main question: What public-purpose land, money, procedure, summary, and filing rules may subdivision regulations address under section 125?
Study summary: This focus is broad but manageable as a single high-level procedural unit. A subdivision regulation may address public-purpose land or money in lieu, regional service commission subdivision powers, tentative-plan fees, street naming, and exemptions. Related rules apply modified subdivision by-law procedures, protect public-purpose land and money uses, require specified consultation and by-law-preliminary procedure before making regulations, allow a written summary for rural plans or amendments, and require the Director to file regulations in the affected county land registration offices without making filing a condition for coming into force.

1. Recall the subdivision regulation subjects in subsection 125(10).
Question: What may a subdivision regulation provide for under subsection 125(10)?
Answer: It may provide for setting aside public-purpose land or paying money in lieu at the Minister discretion, vest specified access and subdivision-plan approval powers in a regional service commission, prescribe tentative-plan approval fees, make subdivision street naming subject to regional service commission approval, and add exemptions under subsection 80(1).
2. Recall how other subdivision rules apply when a subdivision regulation is in effect.
Question: What modified rules apply if a subdivision regulation under section 125 is in effect?
Answer: Section 77 applies with necessary modifications, except that a plan setting aside public-purpose land cannot be approved unless the regional service commission recommends the location and the Minister approves and signs the plan; variance powers under section 78 are vested in the regional service commission or development officer, and sections 79 and 80 apply with necessary modifications.
3. Recall how public-purpose land and money must be handled.
Question: How must public-purpose land and money in lieu be held or used?
Answer: Land vested in the Crown shall be held undeveloped or developed for public purposes and may later be conveyed to a local government if included within its boundaries. Money received in lieu of land or from sale of the land must go into a special account and be spent only to acquire or develop public-purpose land, with investment earnings paid into that account and later local-government transfer rules if applicable.
4. Recall the procedure before the Minister makes a regulation under section 125.
Question: What must the Minister do before making a regulation under section 125?
Answer: Before making a regulation, the Minister shall request views from the regional service commission if the regulation would have effect in a region, request advisory committee views for specified rural district regulations, and follow, with necessary modifications, the preliminary by-law procedure under section 111 except for regulations under paragraph 125(1)(a), (b), (f), or (g).
5. Recall the written-summary and filing rules.
Question: What do subsections 125(15) and 125(16) say about summaries and filing?
Answer: For a zoning regulation or rural plan, section 111 does not require the Minister to make the proposed rural plan or amendment available for public consideration if a written summary is made available. The Director shall file a copy of each regulation in the land registration office for every affected county, but filing is not a condition precedent to the regulation coming into force.

### Official Focused Source
Objection

10(1)A person who wishes to object to the confirmation of a boundary as shown on the plan of survey shall deliver to the Registrar General by personal service, registered mail or courier service before the deadline stipulated in the notice sent under subsection 9(1) or (2), a written statement setting out the reasons for and any grounds and arguments in relation to the objection.

10(2)If a written statement of objection is delivered before the deadline in accordance with subsection (1), the Registrar General shall hold a hearing to determine the validity of the objection and, if applicable, the validity of the applicant’s claim that the boundaries lie as shown on the plan of survey.

10(3)If no written statement of objection is delivered before the deadline in accordance with subsection (1), the Registrar General may

(a)
if satisfied by all the material filed in the proceeding, including the application if applicable, that the boundary or boundaries in question should be confirmed, confirm the location of the boundary or boundaries in question as shown on the plan of survey without holding a hearing, or

(b)
if not satisfied by all the material filed in the proceeding, including the application if applicable, that the boundary or boundaries in question should be confirmed, hold a hearing in accordance with this Act and the regulations.

10(4)Any applicant, any person who delivers a statement of objection under subsection (1) and such other persons as the Registrar General specifies are parties to a hearing held under this section.

10(5)The Registrar General shall cause a notice of hearing to be given, in accordance with subsection (6), to the parties and to such other persons as the Registrar General specifies.

10(6)A notice given under subsection (5) shall specify the date, time and place of the hearing, shall contain a description of the reasons for the making of the application or the initiating of the proceeding, as the case may be, shall set out any grounds and arguments in relation to it, shall describe the reasons and set out the grounds and arguments contained in any objections received and shall otherwise be given in accordance with the regulations.

1994, c.B-7.2, s.10
